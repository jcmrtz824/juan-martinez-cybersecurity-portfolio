# Roadmap: Today → Summer 2027

Built backward from **opening day: Saturday, June 5, 2027** — early enough to own the whole summer, late enough that an April delay doesn't ruin it.

**Today is October 1, 2026. That's 20 months.** Comfortable, but not so comfortable you can start in 2027.

---

## The critical path

Most of these tasks can slip a few weeks without hurting anything. Four cannot:

```
 1.  Confirm the patio can be permitted at all (parking, setbacks)   ← Nov 2026
 2.  Submit the alcohol application via Georgia Tax Center            ← Jan 2027
 3.  Council hearing, IF Kennesaw requires one                        ← Feb–Mar 2027
 4.  Local license issued → state license can then be issued          ← Apr 2027
```

**Item 3 is the wildcard.** If Kennesaw requires council approval, that's 30–60 days and it runs on the council's calendar, not yours. Find out in your first phone call. If the answer is yes, everything moves four to six weeks earlier.

**Item 4 can't be rushed.** Georgia will not issue the state license until Kennesaw has issued the local one. No amount of urgency changes that order.

## The 20-month money picture

| Period | What's earning | Added gross profit/month |
|---|---|---|
| Oct–Dec 2026 | Micheladas + happy hour + Google | **$400–800** |
| Jan–Apr 2027 | Same, plus construction buzz | **$600–1,000** |
| May 2027 | Patio open on beer/micheladas | **$1,500–2,500** |
| Jun 2027 onward | Patio + margaritas, in season | **$4,000–7,000** |

Phase 1 and 2 should generate roughly **$8,000–14,000 of added gross profit** across those 20 months — not enough to fund the build, but enough to cover the licensing, the CPA, the survey and the permits without borrowing. **The quick wins pay for the paperwork.** That's their job.

---

## Three ways to tell it's going wrong early

**1. Micheladas don't sell.** If you can't move 20 micheladas a week at $8 by December, your customers may not be drink buyers. Don't conclude the plan is dead — but shrink the bar, build the cheap patio, and lean harder on food and dead-hour food promotions.

**2. The parking math fails.** If Zoning says 20 outdoor seats require spaces you don't have, you need a variance, a shared-parking agreement, or a 10–12 seat patio. Find this out in **November**, not in January after you've paid for a survey.

**3. The dram shop quote is brutal.** If liquor liability comes back at $8,000/year instead of $2,500, the margarita math changes materially. Get that quote in November so you can re-run the model while it's still cheap to change your mind.

None of the three are reasons to abandon the plan. All three are reasons to **make the phone calls before you spend money** — which is why Phase 1 is all phone calls and no checks.

## If something slips

| If this slips | Do this |
|---|---|
| Alcohol license is late | **Open the patio anyway on beer and micheladas.** The patio earns on its own. Add margaritas when the license lands. |
| Patio construction is late | Open the margaritas indoors first. The license and the patio are separable — *as long as the patio was on the original premises plan.* |
| Council pushes you to a later agenda | Shift everything four to six weeks. June 5 becomes mid-July. Still summer. |
| Money is short | **Build the cheap column** in the patio budget. Umbrellas, pavers, used furniture. Open smaller rather than later. |

**The patio and the liquor license are two separate revenue streams that happen to launch together.** If one is late, launch the other. Don't hold a finished patio hostage to a pending license — that's three months of lost summer for no reason.
