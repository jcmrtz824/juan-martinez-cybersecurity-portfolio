# The Content Playbook

This isn't guesswork. I pulled real **outlier data** — Instagram Reels and TikToks from the last 30 days that massively outperformed their own creator's median — for birria/taqueria content and for michelada/drink content, and then looked at what the winners had in common.

Here's the most important thing it showed.

---

## The finding that should change how you think about this

**The biggest breakouts came from the smallest accounts.**

| Account | Followers | Their normal video | Their breakout | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **@fiestatequilabar** | **4,000** | 2,600 views | **1.9M views** | **706×** |
| **@tmcheladas** | 52,700 | 8,300 views | 1.8M views | **216×** |
| **@chatostacos_** | 32,000 | 16,800 views | 2.7M views | **161×** |
| **@milindobadiraguato** | 33,000 | 33,100 views | 3.8M views | **115×** |
| @soyummy | 3,700,000 | 2,500 views | 2.9M views | 1163× |

Read the first row again. A tequila bar with **four thousand followers** posted one video that did **1.9 million views** — 706 times their own average. @tmcheladas is a michelada vendor whose normal video gets 8,300 views; one of theirs did 1.8 million.

**Nobody in this data broke out because they had a big following. They broke out because they posted the right kind of video, and they posted enough of them to roll the dice.** Your follower count is not the thing standing between you and reach. The number of good attempts you make is.

That's the entire argument for the 30-day shot list.

## What the winners had in common

Six patterns held across nearly every outlier. They're all things you can do with a phone, today, with no editing skill.

### 1. The first second is hands already working. Never a face saying hello.

Every single top performer opened mid-action:

- *"A chef's hand immediately places flat tortillas onto a hot griddle, followed by quick placement of cheese."* — @nightmarket_elgato, 1.8M
- *"Close-up of a gloved hand dipping a red tortilla into a vat of sauce and placing it on a hot flat-top grill."* — @chatostacos_, 2.7M
- *"Close-up of a gloved hand coating a beer can rim in chili powder."* — @tmcheladas, 1.8M

No intro. No "hey guys." No logo. **The action is already happening when the video starts.** That's the whole hook.

### 2. Music only — no talking required

Most winners ran **music with no voiceover at all**. That's great news: you don't need a script, you don't need to be on camera, and you don't have to decide between Spanish and English.

The tracks that showed up were **regional Mexican**:

- *Me Voy Pa'lla* — La Zenda Norteña (@chatostacos_, 2.7M)
- *Como Te Voy a Olvidar* — Los Ángeles Azules (@piecookwithme, 1M)

That's a real, specific finding: **norteño, cumbia and regional Mexican audio is the sound of this niche.** Browse TikTok's audio library for trending regional Mexican tracks and use those.

Where there *was* talking, it was **conversational Spanish over the build** — @tmcheladas just narrates while making the drink. Static camera, voice only. No production at all.

### 3. The camera does not need to move

@tmcheladas: *"Fixed-camera top-down cooking demonstration," "visual_changes: Low (static camera)."* 1.8 million views, 216× their median, from a phone sitting still.

Fast cuts help when you have them. They are **not** required. Don't let editing be the reason you don't post.

### 4. Every restaurant in the data put the address and hours in the caption

This is the pattern that separates restaurants from food influencers, and it's the one most small places miss:

> `📍 4163 S Broadway, Los Angeles, CA 90037`
> `Jue–Dom | 5:00 PM–11:30 PM`
> — @nightmarket_elgato

> `𖡡 55 N. Stephanie St, Suite 120`
> — @chatostacos_

> `📍 837 W Valley Blvd, Colton, CA 92324`
> `#sinaloa #colton #cenaduria`
> — @milindobadiraguato

**Address. Hours. City hashtag. On every single post.** A viral video with no address is a billboard with no exit sign. Notice @milindobadiraguato hashtags their actual town — `#colton`. Yours is `#kennesaw`.

### 5. The money shot is cheese, or a dip, or a pull

The highest-performing single moments in the whole dataset:

- *"Extreme close-up of a crispy wonton being pulled apart, revealing the cheesy, meaty interior"* with *"a loud, satisfying crunch sound effect"* — 3.6M
- Tortilla dipped in consomé, hitting the griddle, cheese melting — 2.7M

**Quesabirria is the single most viral item a taqueria can film.** You have birria. The cheese pull and the consomé dip are your best two seconds of footage and they should appear constantly.

### 6. A question or a challenge in the caption

@fiestatequilabar — the 4,000-follower account that did 1.9M — wasn't a food video at all. It was a bartender trying to land four garnishes in four margaritas, with on-screen text:

> *"If he makes all four in, you owe your friends margaritas!"*

and the caption: *"So who you bringing for margaritas?"*

That's engineered for comments and tags. Somebody tags a friend, the friend watches, TikTok reads that as interest and shows it to more people. **A question in the caption costs nothing and does real work.**

### 7. Effort: "within an hour"

Nearly every outlier was rated as taking **within an hour** to produce. These are not productions. They're a phone, a griddle, and good light.

## Local reach is the only reach that matters to you

A million views from people in Ohio sells zero tacos. 2,000 views from Cobb County fills your dining room. Everything below is for local discovery:

- **Tag the location on every post.** The single most important button on the upload screen.
- **Put the city in the on-screen text and say it out loud.** "Kennesaw." "Cobb County." "Off Cobb Parkway."
- **Name local landmarks.** People search those. Kennesaw State, Town Center, Swift-Cantrell Park.
- **Hashtag your town, like @milindobadiraguato does:**
  `#kennesaw #kennesawga #cobbcounty #marietta #atlantafood #atlfoodie #tacos #taqueria #birria #micheladas #comidamexicana`
- **Reply to every local comment within the first hour.** Comment speed in that first hour is a big part of what decides whether the video keeps getting shown.

A 2,000-view video that's 80% local beats a 50,000-view video that's 2% local. Don't chase the big number.

## Your five formats

Stop inventing. Rotate these. Each is proven in the data above.

| # | Format | Shot | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The griddle** | Meat hitting the plancha, close, loud. 8 sec. | Top hook in every outlier |
| **2** | **The quesabirria** | Dip tortilla → griddle → cheese → fold → pull apart → dip in consomé | The single most viral taqueria item |
| **3** | **The michelada build** | Static camera, top-down. Tajín rim → pour → garnish. | @tmcheladas, 216× their median |
| **4** | **The tray reveal** | Someone walks a loaded tray out of the kitchen. Text: "100% hecho a mano" | @milindobadiraguato, 115× |
| **5** | **The challenge** | A staff bet, a trick shot, a "guess the salsa heat." Question in the caption. | @fiestatequilabar, 706× from 4K followers |

Format 2 is your workhorse. Format 5 is your lottery ticket — film one a week.

## Technical rules, all of them

- **Vertical, 9:16.** Always.
- **Sound on.** The griddle, the chopping, the molcajete, the can opening. Real sound under the music.
- **Close.** Phone about 12 inches from the food. Most restaurant video fails because it's shot from six feet back.
- **Natural light.** Near the door, or outside in shade. Never flash, never fluorescent-only.
- **7–20 seconds** for food. Longer only for a story or a challenge.
- **On-screen text in the first second** — many people watch muted.
- **Loop it.** Several winners were flagged `is_looped: true`. End on the same frame you opened on and people watch twice, which the algorithm counts.
- **Post the same clip to TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook.** One film session, four platforms, 60 extra seconds each.

## Batch filming: the only sustainable way to do this

You run a restaurant. You will not film daily. So don't.

**One 45-minute session on your slowest afternoon:**

1. Set the phone on a cheap tripod near the door, where the light is good
2. Film **8–12 clips** back to back, running the format list
3. Done. That's **two weeks** of daily posts sitting in your camera roll
4. Each day, pick one, add text, add a trending regional Mexican audio, paste your standard caption, post

The caption is the same every time except the first line. Save it in your phone's keyboard shortcuts:

```
[one line about the dish]

📍 [your address], Kennesaw, GA
🕐 [today's hours]
📞 [your phone]

#kennesaw #kennesawga #cobbcounty #atlantafood #atlfoodie
#tacos #taqueria #birria #micheladas #comidamexicana
```

## What to measure

Ignore follower count entirely. Four numbers:

| Metric | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **% of views from Atlanta metro** | TikTok analytics | The only view number that matters |
| **Saves and shares** | TikTok analytics | A save means "I want to go there." Highest intent on the platform. |
| **Your own median views** | Compare your last 10 | The outlier data is all measured against this. Know yours. |
| **"I saw you online"** | Ask at the register | The real conversion number. Write it down. |

Knowing your own median matters more than it sounds: when a video does 5× your median, that's the signal to make three more like it immediately — and you can't see 5× if you don't know what 1× is.

---

## When one takes off

Within the first 24 hours:

1. **Reply to every comment**, local ones first. Keeps it circulating.
2. **Post a follow-up the same day** — same dish, same format, different angle. Some of that audience gets served your next post.
3. **Pin it.** Make sure your bio says Kennesaw, GA and links the website.
4. **Make three more exactly like it.** The instinct after a hit is to try something new. The right move is to repeat it until it stops working.
