# The TikTok Engine: 90 Days of Videos

You already have TikTok. The problem isn't the account, it's the cadence — restaurant accounts die because posting depends on somebody remembering.

This fixes that with one rule: **film in batches, post daily.** One 30-minute session on a slow afternoon makes two weeks of content.

---

## The one thing to understand first

**For a restaurant, views are not the goal. Local views are the goal.**

A video with 40,000 views from people in Ohio sells zero tacos. A video with 900 views from people in Cobb County fills your patio. Everything below is built for local discovery, which means:

- **Say the location out loud and put it in the text.** "Kennesaw," "Cobb County," "off Cobb Parkway," "by Kennesaw State."
- **Name nearby landmarks.** People search those.
- **Tag the location** on every single post. This is the most important button on the upload screen.
- **Reply to local comments immediately.** Comment velocity in the first hour is what decides whether TikTok shows it to more people near you.
- **Hashtags:** `#kennesawga #kennesaw #cobbcounty #atlantafood #atlfoodie #tacos #taqueria #mexicanfood #birria #comidamexicana`

A 2,000-view video that's 80% local beats a 50,000-view video that's 2% local. Don't chase the big number.

---

## Format rules that do most of the work

**Sound on.** The griddle, the chopping, the salsa hitting the molcajete, the tortilla press. ASMR food sound is the single most reliable thing on the platform and it costs you nothing. Don't bury it under music — or use music low, under the real sound.

**Light.** Stand near the door or shoot outside in shade. Never use flash. Never shoot under fluorescents only.

**Close.** Fill the frame with food. Phone about 12 inches away. Most restaurant videos fail because they're shot from six feet back and the food is small.

**Vertical, 9:16.** Always.

**First second decides everything.** Open on the most visual moment — cheese pulling, meat hitting the griddle, salsa pouring. Never open on a person saying "hey guys."

**Length: 7–20 seconds** for food. Longer only if there's a real story.

**Text on screen in the first second.** Many people watch muted. The hook has to be readable.

**Bilingual.** Your market in Kennesaw is both. Alternate — some videos Spanish-first, some English-first, many with both on screen. This roughly doubles your reachable audience and almost nobody does it.

## The seven video types that work for a taqueria

Rotate these. You never need a new idea, just the next one in the rotation.

### 1. The sizzle (your bread and butter)
Meat hitting the hot griddle, loud, close, slow. 8 seconds. No talking.
> Text: **"POV: 4pm en La Fogata"** / **"That sound 🔥"**

### 2. The build
One taco assembled start to finish, overhead, one continuous shot. Tortilla → meat → onion → cilantro → salsa → lime squeeze.
> Text: **"Un taco de birria, paso a paso"** / **"Watch us build it"**

### 3. The cheese pull / the dip
Quesabirria pulled apart. Taco dipped in consomé. Slow motion if your phone does it.
> Text: **"El queso 🧀"** / **"The dip is mandatory"**

### 4. The salsa
Salsa being made in a molcajete. Chiles toasting. The pour.
> Text: **"Salsa casera. Todos los días."** / **"We make it fresh every morning"**

### 5. The drink (start now, grows into 2027)
Michelada being built — Tajín rim, the pour, the garnish. This is your most important recurring video because it trains customers to order drinks.
> Text: **"Michelada preparada, $9"** / **"Cerveza > michelada. Siempre."**

### 6. The family
Your mom's hands making tortillas. Hands only if nobody wants to be on camera. Family-owned is a genuine advantage and this is how you show it without anybody performing.
> Text: **"Family-owned. Kennesaw, GA."** / **"Negocio familiar 🇲🇽"**

### 7. The reply
Somebody comments "what's the best thing on the menu?" — reply with a video. TikTok pushes video replies hard, and it costs you 20 seconds.

## The 90-day calendar

**Post daily if you can, four times a week minimum.** Best windows for a restaurant: **11am–1pm** (lunch decisions) and **4–7pm** (dinner decisions). Consistency matters more than perfect timing.

### Month 1 — build the habit (Oct 2026)

| Week | Film session | Posts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 min: 6 sizzle clips, 4 builds | Daily sizzle/build rotation. Tag Kennesaw on every one. |
| 2 | 30 min: salsa making, 3 micheladas | Launch the michelada videos — these do double duty as a product launch |
| 3 | 20 min: storefront, interior, team hands | Introduce the place. "Family-owned in Kennesaw." |
| 4 | 20 min: cheese pulls, consomé dips | Your highest-performing format. Reply to every comment. |

### Month 2 — find what works (Nov 2026)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 5 | Announce **La Hora Feliz** 3–6pm. Post it 3× in different formats. |
| 6 | Look at your analytics. Find your best video. **Make three more like it.** |
| 7 | Video replies to comments. Behind the scenes: prep, deliveries, open at 7am. |
| 8 | Thanksgiving week — "open while everyone else is closed," family content |

### Month 3 — holiday push (Dec 2026)

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 9 | **Tamales.** If you make them, this is your biggest content and sales moment of the year. Film the whole process. |
| 10 | Posadas, Guadalupe (Dec 12), holiday party catering orders |
| 11 | Nochebuena / Christmas Eve hours. Family content performs hardest here. |
| 12 | Year in review. Thank customers by name. Tease 2027: **"Something's coming this summer 👀"** |

### Beyond: the 2027 build-up

This is the part most businesses miss. **Document the patio and the bar being built.** Weekly, from the first survey stake to opening day.

- "We're building something" (no details, just a shot of the empty lot)
- The pad getting poured
- Railing going up
- First table placed
- First margarita made — *"18 months in the making"*
- Opening day

That series is 20 months of free anticipation, and by the time you open, your local audience already feels invested. Construction content outperforms food content on local accounts because it's a story with an ending people want to see.

## Also post everything to Instagram Reels

Same vertical video, zero extra work. Export without the TikTok watermark and upload to Reels. Different audience, especially slightly older, and Instagram's local discovery for restaurants is strong. Takes 60 seconds per post.

---

## What to measure

Ignore follower count. Watch these:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **% of views from Kennesaw / Atlanta metro** | TikTok shows you this. It's the only view number that matters. |
| **Saves and shares** | Saves mean "I want to go there." Highest-intent signal on the platform. |
| **Profile → website clicks** | Actual traffic you can trace |
| **"I saw you on TikTok"** | Ask at the register. Write it down. This is your real conversion number. |

That last one is the only true measurement. Everything else is a proxy.

---

## When something takes off

If a video goes big, do three things in the first 24 hours:

1. **Reply to every comment**, especially local ones. Keeps it circulating.
2. **Post a follow-up within 24 hours** — same dish, same format, different angle. The algorithm sends some of that audience to your next post.
3. **Pin it** to your profile, and make sure your bio has the website link and says Kennesaw, GA.

Then make three more videos exactly like it. The instinct after a hit is to try something new; the right move is to repeat what worked until it stops working.
