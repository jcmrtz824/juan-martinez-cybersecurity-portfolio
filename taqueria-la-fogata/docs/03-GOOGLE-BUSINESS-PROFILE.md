# Google Business Profile: The Free Thing Worth The Most

If you do one thing from this entire folder, do this. It costs nothing, takes an afternoon, and it is how most of your new customers will find you.

---

## Why this beats TikTok for a taqueria

TikTok reaches people who might want tacos someday. Google reaches people who want tacos **in the next fifteen minutes and are holding their phone.**

Somebody in Kennesaw opens Maps and types "tacos near me." What appears is a ranked list of Google Business Profiles with photos, star ratings, hours, and a Directions button. That list is the most valuable real estate in local restaurant marketing, and there is no charge to be on it.

Three things decide where you rank:

1. **Distance** from the person searching — you can't change this
2. **Relevance** — how completely your profile matches what they searched
3. **Prominence** — reviews, photos, activity, how often people engage

You control two out of three, completely, for free.

## Setup: do this in one sitting

### 1. Claim it

Go to **google.com/business**. Search for "Taqueria La Fogata" — a profile probably already exists, auto-generated from customer activity. Claim it rather than creating a duplicate. Google will verify you, usually by postcard, phone, or video. **Postcards take up to two weeks, so start today.**

If a duplicate listing exists, report it. Duplicates split your reviews and sink you in the rankings.

### 2. Categories — this is the highest-leverage field on the page

- **Primary category: "Mexican Restaurant"** — or **"Taqueria"** if offered. Nothing else.
- **Secondary categories:** Taco Restaurant, Restaurant, Takeout Restaurant, Family Restaurant

The primary category has more influence on what searches you appear in than almost anything else. Don't put something vague like "Restaurant" there.

### 3. Fill in every single field

Google's own data shows complete profiles get dramatically more clicks and direction requests. Empty fields are a ranking signal, and not a good one.

- **Name:** exactly "Taqueria La Fogata" — no keyword stuffing like "Taqueria La Fogata Best Tacos Kennesaw." That's against Google's rules and can get you suspended.
- **Address:** exact, matching your signage and website character-for-character
- **Phone:** your real number, same one that's on the website
- **Website:** the site from [`../website/`](../website/)
- **Hours:** correct, and **update them for every holiday.** Nothing costs you a customer faster than driving there and finding you closed.
- **Attributes:** check everything true — Dine-in, Takeout, Delivery, Serves beer, Good for groups, Family friendly, Casual, Accepts credit cards, Wheelchair accessible, Spanish spoken. **Add "Outdoor seating" the day the patio opens.**
- **Menu:** add it, item by item, with prices. Google reads it and matches it to searches like "birria near me."
- **From the business:** a short description. Family-owned. Kennesaw. What you're known for. Name actual dishes.

### 4. Photos — the biggest lever most restaurants ignore

Profiles with lots of good photos get far more clicks than profiles with a few. Upload **at least 20 to start, then add 2–3 every week, forever.**

| Type | How many | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Food, close up, natural light | 10+ | Your best sellers. One per dish. |
| The storefront | 2–3 | So people recognize it from the road |
| Interior | 2–3 | Clean, lights on, looks open |
| The team | 1–2 | Family-owned is an advantage — show it |
| Drinks | 2–3 | **Micheladas, starting now.** Margaritas in 2027. |
| The patio | as many as possible | **Day one. This is your differentiator.** |

Shoot on a phone, near a window or outside in shade, no flash. Food fills the frame. That's it.

### 5. Turn on messaging and Q&A

Enable messaging if you can answer within a few hours. Then seed the Q&A yourself — you're allowed to ask and answer your own questions, and it's one of the most underused features on the platform:

- "Do you have vegetarian options?"
- "Do you serve beer?" → and in 2027, "Do you have margaritas?"
- "Is there outdoor seating?" → the day the patio opens
- "Do you take large orders for parties?"
- "Is parking available?"
- "Do you speak Spanish?" → "Sí, hablamos español."

## Reviews: the part that compounds

Reviews are the biggest driver of prominence, and prominence is the part of ranking you can actually build.

### Getting them

- **Ask out loud, at the right moment.** When somebody says the food was great — that's the moment. *"Would you mind leaving us a Google review? It really helps a small family business."* That sentence, said sincerely, works better than any sign.
- **Make it one tap.** Google Business Profile gives you a short review link. Turn it into a **QR code**, print it on a small card, and put it with the check and at the register. Cuts the friction to nothing.
- **Target 20+ in your first month, then 10–15/month forever.** Steady beats a burst — Google notices unnatural spikes.
- **Never pay for reviews or offer a discount for one.** It violates Google's policies, it can get your profile suspended, and real reviews from real customers are worth more anyway.

### Responding

**Reply to every review. All of them.** It's a ranking signal and, more importantly, future customers read the replies.

- **Good review:** short, warm, specific, in their language. *"¡Gracias, Maria! Nos alegra que le gustaron los tacos de birria. ¡Hasta la próxima!"*
- **Bad review:** never argue. Thank them, acknowledge it, say what you're doing, invite them back, move it offline. *"Thank you for telling us — we're sorry the wait was long on Saturday. We've added staff for weekend lunch. Please come see us again and ask for me."* That reply is written for the next hundred people who read it, not for the person complaining.

A 4.3★ with thoughtful owner replies converts better than a 4.9★ with silence.

## Google Posts: 10 minutes a week

Posts show up directly on your profile. They expire after about a week, so this is a weekly habit, not a one-time task.

A rotation that works:

- **Monday:** this week's special, with a photo
- **Wednesday:** "La Hora Feliz, 3–6pm — $1 off beer and micheladas"
- **Friday:** weekend dish, best photo you took this week
- **As needed:** holiday hours, new items, patio progress

**Post your patio construction.** Genuinely — people love watching a local place build something. "Week 3: the pad is poured." It builds anticipation for free, and by opening day people already know it's coming.

---

## Weekly routine, 15 minutes

Pick a slow afternoon and do this every week:

- [ ] Reply to every new review
- [ ] Upload 2–3 new photos
- [ ] Post one Google Post
- [ ] Check hours are right for any upcoming holiday
- [ ] Glance at Insights: how many people called, asked for directions, visited the website
- [ ] Answer any new Q&A

That's it. Fifteen minutes a week, permanently, is enough to beat most of your competition on Google — because most of them do nothing at all.

---

## What to watch in Insights

Google gives you real numbers. Four that matter:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **Direction requests** | The purest signal of intent. Somebody asked their phone how to drive to you. |
| **Calls** | Phone orders. Track whether they rise when you post more. |
| **Searches: "discovery" vs "direct"** | Discovery = people who didn't know you and found you. **This is the number that means your profile is working.** |
| **Photo views vs. competitors** | Google shows you this. If you're behind, upload more. |

## Two things not to do

1. **Don't keyword-stuff the business name.** "Taqueria La Fogata Tacos Burritos Kennesaw" will get the profile suspended. The name field is for your name.
2. **Don't let the address, phone, or name differ between Google, your website, Yelp, and Facebook.** Even small differences — "St" vs "Street," a different phone number — confuse Google's confidence that these all describe one business, and it costs you ranking. Pick one exact format and use it everywhere.

---

## After Google, in order

1. **Apple Maps** — free, via Apple Business Connect. Everyone with an iPhone. Most restaurants skip it, which is exactly why it's worth doing.
2. **Yelp** — claim it and keep hours current even if you don't love the platform. It ranks in Google results.
3. **Facebook page** — matters more than you'd think for an older local audience, and it's where community event posts land.

Google first, though. It's not close.
