# The Six Numbers

Ten minutes every Sunday. This is the least exciting document here and probably the most valuable one.

**Why it matters:** in December 2026 you decide whether to spend $15,000 or $40,000 on a patio. The difference between a good decision and a guess is whether you have three months of your own numbers. And you need the food-vs-alcohol split anyway, because the CPA affidavit for the liquor license requires it — so you're tracking this regardless. Starting now just means it's ready when you need it.

---

## The six

### 1. Food % of total sales ⚠️ **license-critical**
`food sales ÷ (food + alcohol sales) × 100`

**Must stay above 50%.** This is a Kennesaw license requirement, certified annually by a CPA, and it also gates your 11am Sunday start. A taqueria clears it easily — but track it monthly so the affidavit is a formality and you'd see a problem coming months out.

### 2. Covers in the dead window (2–5pm, Mon–Thu)
Just count people. A tally sheet by the register is fine.

**This is your most important number.** It's the baseline you'll use to prove — or disprove — that the patio works. You cannot measure improvement against a number you never recorded. Start this week; in December you'll wish you had.

### 3. Drinks per cover
`total drinks sold ÷ total covers`

Today this is beer and sodas. Watch it move as micheladas launch. Every 0.1 it rises is real money. If it's 0.4 now and 0.6 by December, the drink strategy is working and the margarita plan is validated.

### 4. Average ticket
`total sales ÷ number of tickets`

The number the whole plan is trying to raise. Micheladas, happy hour, margaritas, patio — they all show up here.

### 5. Micheladas per week
Your free live experiment. Does your customer buy a drink at $8? If yes, the margarita plan has real evidence behind it. If no, you learned it for $60 of Clamato instead of $40,000 of construction.

### 6. "How did you hear about us?"
Ask. Write down four buckets: **Google / TikTok / drove by / friend.**

Thirty seconds a day, and it tells you where your effort should go. Most restaurants guess at this forever.

## The sheet

Copy this into a notebook or a spreadsheet. One row per week.

```
Week of: __________

 1. Food % of sales ....................... ______%   (must be >50%)
 2. Covers 2-5pm Mon-Thu .................. ______
 3. Drinks per cover ...................... ______
 4. Average ticket ........................ $______
 5. Micheladas sold ....................... ______
 6. Found us via:  Google ____  TikTok ____  Drove by ____  Friend ____

 Total sales this week .................... $______
 Best day / worst day ..................... __________ / __________

```

That last line matters more than it looks. "Nobody sat outside after 7 because there's no light out front" is the kind of observation that saves you a thousand dollars.

---

## Monthly review, 30 minutes

First Sunday of each month:

1. **Is food % still above 50%?** If it ever drops below 55%, pay attention.
2. **Are dead-hour covers trending up?** This is the patio business case being built or broken in real time.
3. **Is average ticket rising?** If not, what did you change and why didn't it work?
4. **Where are customers coming from?** Put more effort into whichever channel is winning.
5. **Update the model.** Open [04-MONEY-MODEL.md](./04-MONEY-MODEL.md) and replace one of my **[ASSUMPTION]** figures with your real one. By December, most of that model should be yours instead of mine.

## What to do at the December decision point

Bring three months of this sheet and answer four questions honestly:

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Micheladas selling 25+/week? | Customers buy drinks. Margarita plan is validated. | Shrink the bar budget. Lean on food. |
| Dead-hour covers up 30%+? | People will come at 4pm. Build the patio. | Find out why. Is it the offer, or the hours? |
| Food % comfortably above 50%? | Licensing path is clear. | Fix this before applying. |
| Did Zoning confirm the parking works? | Proceed to permits. | Solve parking, or build a 12-seat patio. |

**Four yeses: commit the money, build the mid-range patio.**
**Two or three yeses: build the cheap patio** and let it prove itself.
**One or none: don't spend $40,000 yet.** Keep running quick wins, re-measure in March, and push opening to late summer or 2028. The plan isn't wrong — the timing is.

That last row is the real reason to track anything. Not so you can admire a spreadsheet, but so that if this isn't working you find out in December when it costs you nothing, instead of in August 2027 when it costs you everything.
