# Selling More Per Ticket

Content brings people in. This is about what happens after they sit down — and it's the cheapest revenue in the entire business, because the customer is **already there**. No ad, no video, no new cover. Just one more item on the ticket.

---

## The michelada attach rate

You already sell micheladas. So the question isn't whether to — it's **what fraction of your beers go out as micheladas**, and whether anybody's asking.

The margin, for reference:

| | Plain beer | Michelada |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $5.00 | $8.00 |
| Your cost | $0.92 | $1.60 |
| **Your profit** | **$4.08** | **$6.40** |

**+$2.32 per beer converted**, for about 68¢ of Clamato, lime, hot sauce and Tajín.

What the attach rate is worth, per week:

| Beers/week | 20% become micheladas | 50% | 80% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | +$23 | +$58 | +$93 |
| 100 | +$46 | +$116 | +$186 |
| 200 | +$93 | +$232 | +$371 |

At 100 beers a week, moving from 20% to 50% is **+$70/week — about $3,600 a year** from one sentence asked more often.

### The sentence

> **"¿Se la preparo como michelada?"**
> **"Want me to make that a michelada?"**

Asked **every time** somebody orders a beer. Not sometimes. Every time.

That's it. That's the whole mechanism. Most places leave this money on the counter because nobody decided it was somebody's job.

### Make it easy to say yes

- **Three options, three prices, one card.** Chelada $7 · Michelada $8 · Preparada $9. On the table and at the register, with a photo.
- **The photo does the selling.** A loaded michelada with a Tajín rim and a cucumber spear sells itself; a word on a menu doesn't.
- **Keep the station ready** — Clamato, Tajín, limes, hot sauce within arm's reach of where beer is poured. If building one is a trip across the kitchen, your staff will stop offering during a rush.
- **Track it.** Count micheladas vs. beers weekly. If the rate drops, somebody stopped asking.

## The elote attach

Elote is close to a perfect add-on: cheap, fast, visual, shareable, and nobody orders it on purpose unless reminded.

- Costs you roughly **$1.20**, sells at **$4.00** → **$2.80 profit, 70% margin**
- No griddle time competing with tacos
- Photographs better than almost anything else you sell — which is why it's in the shot list repeatedly

> **"¿Le agrego un elote?"** / **"Add an elote?"**

At 30 extra elotes a week that's **+$84/week, about $4,400/year.**

It's also the right thing to give away for the code word in [04-TURN-VIEWS-INTO-VISITS.md](./04-TURN-VIEWS-INTO-VISITS.md): cheap enough to be painless, good enough that people order one next time.

## The combo that raises the whole ticket

Don't sell items. Sell **the move**.

> **"La Combinación"** — 3 tacos + elote + michelada — **$21**

À la carte that's $10.50 + $4 + $8 = $22.50, so the customer saves $1.50 and feels smart. You get:

| | À la carte, if they only bought tacos | The combo |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10.50 | $21.00 |
| Your cost | ~$3.15 | ~$5.95 |
| **Profit** | **$7.35** | **$15.05** |

**Double the profit from the same customer**, for $1.50 of discount. Put it on a table card, name it, and let the staff point at it instead of explaining it.

This also solves a real problem: people who've seen your TikTok and don't know what to order. "La Combinación" is an answer, and it's the answer you want.

## The seven sentences

Print these. Tape them where only staff can see.

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| Any beer ordered | "¿Se la preparo como michelada?" |
| Tacos ordered, no side | "¿Le agrego un elote?" |
| Birria ordered | "El consomé va aparte, sí?" |
| Someone can't decide | "La Combinación. Tres tacos, elote y michelada." |
| Water or soda ordered | "¿Horchata? La hacemos aquí." |
| Family or group | "¿Una orden de guacamole para la mesa?" |
| Paying, looked happy | "¿Nos deja una reseña en Google? Nos ayuda mucho." |

That last one isn't about the ticket — it's about the next hundred customers. Google reviews are the biggest driver of local search ranking, and the moment somebody says the food was great is the only moment that ask ever works.

## What this is worth together

Conservative, at your current volume:

| Lever | Added profit/week | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Michelada attach, 20% → 50% (100 beers/wk) | +$70 | +$3,600 |
| 30 more elotes/week | +$84 | +$4,400 |
| 10 combos/week instead of taco-only | +$77 | +$4,000 |
| **Total** | **+$231** | **~$12,000** |

**No new customers. No ad spend. No construction.** Three sentences asked consistently and one table card.

And it stacks with the content work: content brings more people, and every one of them walks into a system that sells them more. Doing both is worth more than the sum, which is why they're in the same plan.

---

## Two cautions

**Don't push.** Offering once is service. Offering twice is pressure, and it costs you the repeat visit. One ask, cheerful, then drop it.

**Watch your food-vs-alcohol ratio.** If you ever pursue the liquor license in [`later/`](./later/), Kennesaw requires at least 50% of sales to come from food, certified by a CPA. A taqueria clears that easily — but if micheladas ever grow into a serious share of revenue, keep an eye on it. Track it in [06-TRACKING.md](./06-TRACKING.md).
