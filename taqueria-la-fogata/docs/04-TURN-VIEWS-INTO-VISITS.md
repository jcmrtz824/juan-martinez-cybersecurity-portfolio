# Turning Views Into Visits

This is where most restaurant content dies. An account gets decent views and sales don't move, so the owner concludes content doesn't work. Usually the content worked fine — the **bridge** was missing.

A view becomes a customer only if three things are true:

1. They know **where you are** (and it's close enough)
2. They know **what to order** — one specific thing they now want
3. There's a reason to come **soon**, not "sometime"

Most posts deliver none of the three.

---

## Fix 1 — Address and hours on every post

Every restaurant in the outlier data did this. Not some. All of them.

> `📍 4163 S Broadway, Los Angeles, CA 90037`
> `Jue–Dom | 5:00 PM–11:30 PM`

Save this in your phone as a keyboard shortcut so it's one tap:

```
📍 [address], Kennesaw, GA 30144
🕐 [hours]
📞 [phone]

#kennesaw #kennesawga #cobbcounty #atlantafood #atlfoodie
#tacos #taqueria #birria #micheladas #comidamexicana
```

On iPhone: Settings → General → Keyboard → Text Replacement. Make the shortcut `;;fog`. Now your full caption block is three keystrokes.

**A viral video with no address is a billboard with no exit sign.**

---

## Fix 2 — Sell one thing, not the menu

"Come try our food" gives someone nothing to act on. "Quesabirria, $13.50, dipped in free consomé" creates a specific craving with a specific price attached.

Every post should make **one item** the hero. That's why the shot list is one dish per day.

The order people can repeat without thinking is the order they place. Name it, price it, done.

---

## Fix 3 — The code word (this is how you measure content)

Here's the honest problem: you will never know for certain whether a video sold a taco. Unless you build a way to find out.

**The code word system:**

1. In one post a week, say: **"Say FOGATA at the register for a free elote."**
2. Put it in the on-screen text and the caption.
3. Tell your staff: when somebody says it, **make a tally mark.**
4. Count them Sunday.

That tally is the only hard number connecting content to sales that a small restaurant can actually get. An elote costs you about $1.20 — so twenty redemptions cost you $24 and tell you that at minimum twenty people walked in **because of a video**. That's worth far more than $24.

Rotate the giveaway so it stays cheap: free elote, free consomé, free chips and salsa, $1 off a michelada. Change the word monthly (`FOGATA`, `BIRRIA`, `KENNESAW`) so you can tell which month's content is pulling.

**Do not make the giveaway big.** The point is measurement, not a discount program. Free elote is plenty.

---

## Fix 4 — Urgency, but real

"Come by sometime" loses to "today only." But don't manufacture fake scarcity — people see through it and it costs you trust.

Real urgency you actually have:

- **"Birria's on until we run out."** True at most taquerias, and it works.
- **"Weekend special, Saturday and Sunday only."**
- **"Open late tonight."**
- **"Tamales — December only."** Seasonal is the strongest honest urgency there is.
- **"First 20 people today."**

One a week is enough. If everything is urgent, nothing is.

---

## Fix 5 — Answer every comment, and answer DMs like a host

Comments do double duty: they tell the algorithm the video is worth showing, and they're where a maybe becomes a customer.

- **"Where is this?"** → full address, plus "we're open till 9 tonight 👋" — never just the address
- **"What should I order?"** → one answer, not five. *"Quesabirria and a michelada. Trust me."*
- **"Do you have vegetarian?"** → *"Sí — nopal tacos, and the elote. Both great."*
- Somebody tags a friend → **reply to it.** *"Bring them 👀"* Tags are the highest-value comment on the platform; acknowledge them.

**Reply within the first hour when you can.** Comment velocity in that window is a big part of what decides whether the video keeps getting pushed.

---

## Fix 6 — Make sure the landing works

When somebody decides to come, don't lose them on the last step.

- [ ] **TikTok bio:** "Taqueria La Fogata · Kennesaw, GA" + website link
- [ ] **Website link in bio** — the one in [`../website/`](../website/)
- [ ] **Google Business Profile claimed and accurate** — most people who see your video will Google you before driving. If the hours there are wrong, you lose them. → [03-GOOGLE-BUSINESS-PROFILE.md](./03-GOOGLE-BUSINESS-PROFILE.md)
- [ ] **Hours correct everywhere**, especially holidays. Nothing kills a new customer like a locked door.
- [ ] **Pin your best video** to the top of the profile

That Google step matters more than it looks. The sequence for a real customer is usually: *see video → Google the name → look at photos and hours → drive over.* Your TikTok creates the intent; your **Google profile closes it.** If content is the only thing you fix, you'll leak most of the customers it creates.

## The funnel, with honest numbers

Rough but useful for a local restaurant account:

```
10,000 views
   ↓  ~15% are actually local                  →  1,500 local views
   ↓  ~3% look you up                          →     45 profile/Google visits
   ↓  ~20% of those come in within two weeks   →      9 visits
   ↓  average ticket ~$22                       →   $198
```

**About $200 of revenue per 10,000 local-weighted views.** That sounds small, and it's why the people who win at this are the ones who post 30 times a month instead of three. Thirty posts averaging 3,000 views is 90,000 views — and the real prize is that one of those thirty is the 100× outlier.

Two things improve this funnel far more than extra views:

- **Raising the local share** from 15% to 50% — all it takes is location tags, city hashtags, and saying "Kennesaw" out loud. This is the single biggest lever on the page.
- **Fixing the landing** — Google profile, correct hours, pinned video. Free, one afternoon.
