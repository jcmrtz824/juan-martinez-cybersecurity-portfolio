# Getting Licensed to Pour in Kennesaw, Georgia

> **This is a research summary, not legal advice.** Alcohol licensing is local, it changes, and some Kennesaw city pages were unreachable from where I did this research. Everything below is either from a verifiable source (cited at the bottom) or clearly flagged as unconfirmed. **The city's answer always beats mine.** Use the phone scripts in Part 5 — that is the point of this document.

---

## Part 1: The thing most people get wrong about Georgia

Georgia is a **local-control** state. There is no single "Georgia liquor license" you apply for and receive.

**You need two licenses: a City of Kennesaw license AND a Georgia state license.** And the order is fixed:

> **The local license comes first. The state will not issue yours until Kennesaw has issued theirs.**

The good news: since August 2020, Georgia runs **centralized alcohol licensing**. You file **one application through the Georgia Tax Center (GTC)**, and it routes to both Kennesaw and the state Department of Revenue. You don't file twice. But Kennesaw still has to approve before the state finalizes, so **your timeline is governed by Kennesaw, not by Atlanta.**

Practical consequence: every time you want to know how long this takes or what it costs, the right phone number is the **City of Kennesaw**, not the state.

## Part 2: What you're applying for

You want **margaritas**, which means tequila, which means **distilled spirits for consumption on the premises** — commonly called a **pouring license**. Kennesaw publishes a document by that name ("Pouring License") and an "Alcoholic Beverage License Application."

Note the two different things both called a "permit," because people mix them up constantly:

| | What it is | Who gets it | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alcohol license** | The *business's* right to sell | Taqueria La Fogata, the business | City of Kennesaw + GA DOR, via Georgia Tax Center |
| **Pouring permit** | An *individual employee's* right to serve | Every server/bartender, each one | **Kennesaw Police Department** |

You need the license once. You need a pouring permit for **every single person who serves a drink**, and a new one every time you hire.

### Micheladas: already covered

You already sell micheladas under your existing beer license, so this isn't an open question. Noting it here only because it establishes a useful precedent for the conversation with the city: beer plus non-alcoholic mixers sits inside a malt beverage license.

What that does **not** cover is tequila. A margarita is distilled spirits, and that's the whole reason this document exists.

For selling *more* micheladas — the attach rate, which is where the money actually is — see [05-SELL-MORE-PER-TICKET.md](../05-SELL-MORE-PER-TICKET.md).

## Part 3: The requirements you need to plan around

### The 50% food rule — the one that can cost you the license

Georgia defines an **"eating establishment"** as one licensed to sell alcohol that derives **at least 50% of its total annual gross food and beverage sales from food.**

Kennesaw enforces this with a **notarized "Food Sales and Alcohol Beverages Sales Affidavit" that must be completed by a CPA** and submitted with your pouring license application. This is a confirmed Kennesaw requirement, not a general one.

The same 50% threshold also gates **Sunday sales**: Kennesaw voters approved Georgia's Sunday brunch provision (the "Mimosa Mandate"), which lets restaurants start alcohol service at **11:00am instead of 12:30pm on Sundays** — but only establishments making at least 50% of revenue from food qualify.

**What this means for you in practice:**

- A taqueria is food-dominant by nature. You will very likely clear 50% comfortably. This is not a reason for concern.
- But it is an **annual, auditable, CPA-certified number.** You need to be tracking it monthly so the affidavit is a formality, not a scramble.
- It also sets a hard ceiling on how aggressive your bar can get. If margaritas ever grow to where alcohol passes 50% of sales, you have a license problem, not a success. Realistically: not going to happen at a taco shop, but know the line exists.
- **Find a CPA who has done a Kennesaw alcohol affidavit before.** Ask the Business License Office if they can tell you which local CPAs file these regularly.

### Background checks and personal eligibility

Kennesaw PD runs **GCIC background checks** and issues pouring permits. The published disqualifiers:

- **No felony convictions in the last 5 years**
- **Not currently on alcohol/drug probation**
- **Two or more alcohol or drug convictions in the last five years = denial**

Check this for yourself and for every family member who will be named on the license — **before** you spend money on CPAs and site plans. If there's an issue, it is far better to find out in month one.

### Mandatory alcohol training

All new applicants and existing licensees must attend an **alcohol sales and service workshop approved by the Business License Manager.** Kennesaw names **TIPS** (Health Communications Inc.) as an example — choose the **on-premise** option for a pouring license.

Budget ~$40/person. Do it for everyone, not just the minimum. In Georgia, over-serving a visibly intoxicated person is your liability, and documented training is your defense.

### Distance requirements

Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 3-3-21) restricts alcohol sales near churches, schools, and some other sites, and cities layer their own rules on top. Since you're an existing business already selling beer at a fixed location, you're probably fine — but **distilled spirits sometimes carry different distance rules than beer.** Confirm it. If a church or school went in near you after you opened, ask specifically.

### Council approval and a public hearing

Many Georgia cities require the mayor and council to approve new alcohol licenses, which can mean a **posted notice and a public hearing**, adding **30–60 days** to the timeline.

I could not confirm whether Kennesaw requires this. **Ask — it's question #6 below.** If the answer is yes, it is the single biggest item in your schedule and everything in the roadmap shifts around it.

### Taxes you'll start owing

Kennesaw's Business License Department collects **Liquor by the Drink tax** and **Alcoholic Beverage Excise Tax** in addition to your license fee. These are ongoing filings, usually monthly.

Get the forms and the due dates **at the same time you get the license**. A missed excise filing is a stupid way to put a license at risk, and it happens to new licensees constantly.

## Part 4: ⚠️ The sequencing trap — read this twice

**Your liquor license covers a specific, defined, city-approved "licensed premises."**

If you get licensed for the indoor dining room in early 2027, and then build the patio, **the patio is not covered.** You cannot legally serve a margarita on it. You would have to go back to the city and amend the licensed premises: new site plan, new review, new fee, more waiting — possibly through the same council process all over again.

**This is the most expensive avoidable mistake in the entire project.**

**What to do instead:** when you make your very first call to the city, say that you are planning **both** a pouring license **and** a new outdoor patio, and ask how to get them reviewed together. Put the patio on the site plan you submit with the alcohol application, even if it isn't built yet. Ask the Business License Office *and* Planning & Zoning the same question, because they may give different answers and you want both on record.

Related: most Georgia jurisdictions require a patio where alcohol is served to be a **defined, physically delineated area** — which is why there's a railing/fence line in the patio budget. That fence isn't decoration, it's the legal boundary of your licensed premises. Confirm the exact requirement (height, material, whether gates need to be self-closing) before you buy anything.

## Part 5: The phone calls — scripts to use

### Call 1 — City of Kennesaw, Business License Office

**3080 Moon Station Rd., Kennesaw, GA 30144**
**770-429-4540** (one source also listed 770-424-8274 — try both)

> "Hi, I own Taqueria La Fogata in Kennesaw. We currently have a beer license. We're planning to add distilled spirits — margaritas — and build an outdoor patio, targeting summer 2027. I'd like to understand the process. Can I ask a few questions?"

### Call 2 — Kennesaw Planning & Zoning: 770-590-8268

> "I own a restaurant in Kennesaw and I want to add an outdoor dining patio, about 400 square feet, 20 seats, on the side of the building. We'll be serving alcohol on it."

1. What's our zoning classification, and is outdoor dining permitted by right or does it need a variance or special use permit?
2. Do we need a formal site plan review? What has to be on the plan, and do you need a stamped survey?
3. Does adding 20 outdoor seats change our required parking count? ← *this one kills patios. Ask it early.*
4. What are the setback requirements from the property line?
5. What's the current review timeline?
6. Is there anything about serving alcohol outdoors that changes the zoning review?

### Call 3 — Kennesaw Building Services: 770-429-4554

1. What permits do we need for a patio with a concrete pad, railing, lighting and shade structure?
2. Does a pergola or roof change the permit type or trigger a structural review?
3. What are the ADA access requirements for the patio?
4. Will this require a new or updated Certificate of Occupancy?
5. What inspections, in what order?

### Call 4 — Kennesaw Police Department (employee pouring permits)

Kennesaw PD handles GCIC background checks and pouring permits by appointment.

1. How do we schedule pouring permit appointments for our staff?
2. Cost per permit, and how long are they valid?
3. How far ahead of opening should staff get permitted?
4. Does the owner need one too?

### Call 5 — Your insurance agent

> "We're adding distilled spirits service and an outdoor patio in 2027. I need to understand how that changes our coverage."

1. What does **liquor liability / dram shop** coverage cost for us? ← *get this number early; it's the most commonly underestimated line in the budget*
2. Does the patio change our general liability or property premium?
3. Does documented TIPS training for all staff reduce the premium?
4. What do we need to tell you about, and when?

## Part 6: The sequence, in order

```
 1.  Check eligibility (felony/probation) for everyone on the license   ← free, do first
 2.  Call 1: Business License Office — get fees, timeline, patio-together answer
 3.  Call 2 + 3: Zoning and Building — confirm patio is permittable AND parking works
 4.  Call 5: Insurance — get the dram shop number
      ─── decision point: do the real numbers still work? ───
 5.  Engage a CPA who has filed a Kennesaw alcohol affidavit
 6.  Survey + site plan, WITH the patio on it
 7.  Submit patio permits to Building Services
 8.  Submit ONE alcohol application via Georgia Tax Center (routes to city + state)
 9.  Council hearing, if required
10.  Build patio; staff do TIPS; staff get pouring permits from Kennesaw PD
11.  Kennesaw issues the local license
12.  Georgia DOR finalizes the state license   ← cannot happen before step 11
13.  Final inspections + updated Certificate of Occupancy
14.  Set up Liquor by the Drink + excise tax filings
15.  Soft launch, then open
```

Steps 1–4 cost almost nothing and answer whether this project is viable. **Do them in October 2026.** Do not spend money on step 5 or later until step 4 is done.
