## The three things worth knowing now

Even while this is on the back burner, these three facts are worth carrying around.

**1. The patio has to be on the license from the start.** A Georgia liquor license covers a specific, city-approved *licensed premises*. If you license the indoor room and then build a patio, the patio isn't covered — you can't serve a margarita on it — and amending the premises means a new site plan, new review, and possibly another council hearing. Whenever you make that first call to the city, mention **both** the pouring license and the patio.

**2. Parking is what kills patios.** Adding 20 outdoor seats can raise your required parking count under Kennesaw zoning. That's a free phone call to Planning & Zoning (770-590-8268) and it's worth making long before you pay anyone for a survey.

**3. The 50% food rule.** Kennesaw requires a CPA-notarized affidavit showing at least half your food-and-beverage sales come from food. A taqueria clears this comfortably, but it's an annual certified number — so the food-% line in [`../06-TRACKING.md`](../06-TRACKING.md) is worth keeping even now. It costs nothing to track and it's already required reading if you ever file.

## What's unconfirmed

Georgia is a local-control state, and several City of Kennesaw and Georgia Department of Revenue pages were unreachable during research. These are flagged throughout [LIQUOR-LICENSE-KENNESAW.md](./LIQUOR-LICENSE-KENNESAW.md) and still need a phone call:

- Kennesaw's exact pouring license fee (the ~$3,000 figure is a third-party aggregator, not the city)
- Whether a new license requires mayor and council approval
- The exact patio enclosure spec for serving alcohol outdoors
- Kennesaw's Liquor by the Drink tax rate

One afternoon of calls settles all of it, and the scripts are written. No rush — just don't spend money before making them.
